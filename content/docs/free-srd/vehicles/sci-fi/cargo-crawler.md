# Cargo Crawler

A cargo crawler is less a vehicle than a piece of the yard that happens to move.
It runs the same circuit between the landing field and the stacks every shift,
at a speed that lets a person walk alongside it, and it has been doing so for
long enough that nobody at the yard looks at it any more.

That is the whole of its value at a table. It is slow, it is blind, it is
enormous, and it is *scenery* — which makes it the best cover, the best
distraction and the worst thing to be standing in front of on the entire dock.

## Tactics

A crawler is not driven at anyone; it is *aimed*, several rounds early, and then
it is somebody else's problem. Ten dice of collision damage is not a threat to
be resolved with a dodge — the target moves or the target is gone — and the
crawler takes nothing back from anything smaller than itself.

Against the party, the interesting turn is never the impact. It is the four
rounds beforehand, spent deciding whether to deal with the driver, the clamps,
or the twenty-foot blind band where the cab cannot see anyone at all.

## Encounter Notes

**Found** — Landing fields, container stacks, refinery lines, mining strips, and
anywhere cargo moves in six-container lots rather than by hand.

**Who Has Them** — The yard. Crawlers belong to operations, not to people, and a
stolen one is missed within the hour because the circuit it should be on is
empty.

**Cover, Not Transport** — Most parties will use a crawler without ever starting
one. It is a moving wall the length of ten spaces, it is already going where the
yard goes, and riding the deck costs nothing but the two rounds of Cold Start
that a getaway would have cost anyway.
