# Freighters

Cargo is why anybody is out here. These two ships are the ends of that trade:
one small enough that a party can own it and large enough that it can never be
owned by anyone the party will meet.

The light freighter is written to be *theirs* — patched, indebted, and unreliable
in a specific documented way. The bulk hauler is written to be robbed.

## Choosing Between Them

**Light Freighter** is the party's address. Everything on the card is chosen to
generate scenes rather than to win them: Object DEF 18 in forty mismatched
panels, two tons of hold that is not on any manifest, and a cold start that
fails on a target 10 KNO check at the worst available moment. It is unarmed with
both hardpoints capped and wired, which is an invitation rather than an
oversight.

**Bulk Hauler** is a location with a schedule. Nobody defends it, because
defending it was never the plan — the plan is that it takes ten minutes to start
and cannot enter atmosphere, so there is nowhere to take it. Twenty containers
ride an open spine in hard vacuum with a crew of four in one compartment at the
far end, and the entire heist is about reaching it rather than about anyone
aboard.

## Encounter Notes

**Found** — Every port, every lane, every dock. Freight is the background traffic
of a spacefaring setting and both of these are meant to be unremarkable until the
party has a reason to care.

**Who Has Them** — Light freighters belong to their crews, or to the lender who
is about to repossess one. Bulk haulers belong to shipping lines, and the four
people aboard are employees with no stake in the cargo whatsoever — which is the
most useful fact on the card.

**The Ten Minutes** — Any plan involving a hauler is a plan about its ignition.
It cannot be flown away in a firefight, so the party is stealing *from* it: a
container, a manifest, or whoever is riding in the crew module. Release a clamp
and the container leaves on the hauler's own vector, which is the only theft on
this page that requires no piloting at all.
