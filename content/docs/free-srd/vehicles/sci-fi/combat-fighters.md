# Combat Fighters

The two ships below are the ones a party meets when somebody official has
decided the situation is over. They are almost never the party's own, and the
question at the table is rarely how to beat them — it is how long the party has
before the second one arrives.

They divide the job cleanly. The **Interceptor** finds you and keeps finding
you. The **Patrol Gunship** takes its time and lands people on your hull.

## Choosing Between Them

**Interceptor** is a clock, not an opponent. It is faster than anything else in
this section, it carries one person, and it cannot board, hold or arrest
anybody. What it does is arrive first and refuse to lose the party — which turns
every subsequent scene into a question of how many rounds are left.

**Patrol Gunship** is the consequence. It is slower than the ship it is chasing
and that does not matter, because the collar only needs to touch once. The
turret is the detail worth noticing: a gunner who is not the pilot fires without
the driver's *minor disadvantage*, so a gunship shoots meaningfully better than
an interceptor while flying meaningfully worse.

Run them together and the pairing does the work by itself — one ship that cannot
be escaped and one that cannot be survived, arriving several rounds apart.

## Encounter Notes

**Found** — Patrol lanes, customs cordons, restricted approaches, and about four
rounds after any alarm anyone raises in a system with a garrison in it.

**Who Has Them** — System patrol, corporate security fleets, and the occasional
salvaged hull with the transponder cut out and nobody aboard who is entitled to
be flying it.

**Against the Party** — Neither is a fair fight and neither is meant to be. A
party's answer to an interceptor is cover, atmosphere or a dock; their answer to
a gunship is to not be aboard when the collar mates. Both are problems of
geography rather than of damage.
