> [!NOTE]
> **Short Version:**
> Count everything in a character's favor as +1 and everything against them as -1, then add it all together:
> - **+2 or more** = Major Advantage — roll 3d6
> - **+1** = Minor Advantage — roll 2d6, +2
> - **0** = Neutral — roll 2d6
> - **-1** = Minor Disadvantage — roll 2d6, -2
> - **-2 or less** = Major Disadvantage — roll 1d6

Advantage is a special type of modifier that can be added to any decision roll by the GM based on the situation faced by the characters. Unlike most modifiers which only provide a bonus or penalty, advantage can also alter the number of decision dice rolled. This allows the GM to quickly alter the difficulty of a decision roll on the fly.

Advantage works as a scale rather than a switch. Every effect that applies to a roll is worth points on that scale, the points are added together, and the total decides what is rolled.

## Counting It Up

The GM decides which effects apply. Only then are they counted.

**Every effect that applies is worth one point** in the direction it pushes — in the character's favor, or against them. A few weightier effects state that they grant a *major* advantage or disadvantage on their own; those are worth two points. Add every point together and read the total off the scale:

| Total | Roll | Called |
|---|---|---|
| **+2 or more** | 3d6, plus 1 for every point past +2 | Major Advantage |
| **+1** | 2d6, +2 | Minor Advantage |
| **0** | 2d6 | Neutral |
| **-1** | 2d6, -2 | Minor Disadvantage |
| **-2 or less** | 1d6, minus 1 for every point past -2 | Major Disadvantage |

Nothing is wasted and nothing cancels twice. Three advantages against one disadvantage totals +2, which is still a Major Advantage. A Major Disadvantage answered by one advantage slides back up to a Minor Disadvantage. Stacking beyond the ends of the scale keeps paying, a point at a time.

The major levels add or remove a decision die, and that die *replaces* the flat bonus or penalty rather than joining it — a Major Advantage rolls 3d6 with no +2, and a Major Disadvantage rolls 1d6 with no -2.

**Neutral** is the starting point of the scale and the standard for when nothing applies or the two sides balance out. Advantage is most often earned through equipment, character features, and tactical decisions; disadvantage through conditions, poor equipment, failed rolls, and tactical decisions during play.

## Gaining Advantage

Some abilities and effects grant advantage, particularly in combat. **An effect is named for what it grants on its own** — an effect that grants a minor advantage is worth one point, and an effect that grants a major advantage is worth two. An effect that simply "grants advantage" without saying which is worth one point.

This means an effect never guarantees the roll it names. It moves the scale in the user's favor by the points it is worth, and everything else in play is counted alongside it. An ability granting a major advantage into the teeth of a minor disadvantage leaves the roll at a minor advantage — the ability did its work, the situation ate some of it. All advantages and disadvantages must be revealed before the dice have been rolled, including those from abilities.

## Special Notes for the Game Master:

- All rolls start as neutral. Don't be afraid to keep them there.
- Decide what applies before counting anything. Only include things that would have an effect on their own, do not include items that only matter when they add up. This can help limit the number of effects that cause advantage and keep it more narratively consistent.
- Do not include things that already grant a bonus or penalty. For example, fatigued characters are not held at a disadvantage because the fatigued condition applies its own penalties.
- The scale runs in both directions without a limit, but the count is not an accounting exercise. Most rolls should land between +1 and -1, and a total past +2 or -2 should mean the situation genuinely got out of hand.
- Talk with the rest of the table when determining advantage, it is everyone's responsibility to announce effects. It is the GM's responsibility to determine the balance of the situation and whatever advantage it may grant.

## Advantage and Contested Rolls

It is best practice to avoid handing out disadvantage during a contested roll if something could reasonably be an advantage to the other party instead. If something acts as a disadvantage for one contestant it acts as an advantage for the other. Treat advantage for contested rolls as if the two parties occupy the scale at either end, competing for having the advantage in their favor.
