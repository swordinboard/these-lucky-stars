> [!NOTE]
> The rules for squeezing through a tight space are primarily designed for chases and spaces not intended to be moved through swiftly. As such, passing through a tight space acts as a form of difficult terrain when the terrain presents a potential hazard of some kind.
>
> A typical 3ft hallway should not present a hazard to a medium character whose width is only 2ft, but a downed tree propped 3ft off the ground or a 3ft opening in a rocky cave should because they do present potential hazards. Keep this in mind when determining how a creature or character should be able to move through a space.

A space that is smaller than one's footprint but larger than their expected width can usually be passed through as part of a move action, however it may still count as a form of difficult terrain for combat purposes. Allowing a character or creature to occupy such a space at the end of their turn is left to GM discretion and may result in a minor disadvantage to physical actions due to the limited space.

Moving through a space that is in any dimension equal to or smaller than one's expected width requires a target 8 AGI check, always counts as difficult terrain, and requires double the movement distance cost; effectively lowering the total travel distance available for that movement. Failing this check results in getting stuck halfway in the opening and the [Pinned](/docs/free-srd/core-rules/wounds--conditions/#pinned) (Body) condition, which reduces AGI and Speed to 0 (if AGI is negative before the condition, the negative penalty remains). A target 8 STR or AGI check can then be used to free oneself and remove the condition for 1 AP. How far into the opening or in which direction one frees themselves is left to GM discretion.

Spaces that are smaller than half a creature or character's expected width cannot be squeezed through in this manner.
