A spacecraft is a vehicle and uses the [Vehicle Rules](/docs/free-srd/core-rules/vehicle-rules/) unchanged. It is far larger than anything else those rules are usually pointed at, so four things are worth saying before reading one of these cards.

**Size is still the longest dimension in 5ft spaces.** A 90ft freighter is Size 18, and [collision damage](/docs/free-srd/core-rules/vehicle-rules/#collision-damage) is still 1d6 per space. The dice counts get large on purpose — that is the rule scaling, not a special case, and the collision rules already name a 16-space ship. In practice a ship never rolls collision against a person; it rolls against a dock, a gantry, or another ship, and the GM narrates what happened to anyone standing between them.

**The Speed line is a maneuvering speed.** It is what the ship does in a round while somebody is shooting at it, threading a debris field, or trying to put down on a pad it does not fit. Getting from one world to the next is not measured in rounds and no number on the card describes it — that distance is a scene transition, a fuel problem, or a countdown, and it belongs to the GM rather than to the movement rules. Where a ship handles differently in atmosphere, the card says so on the same line.

**Weapons are crew-served.** A ship's guns are not the ship's attack; they are a weapon an occupant fires, spending their own AP and rolling their own attack on their own turn. Two consequences follow from rules already in play. A pilot who fires while flying is taking [an action other than Drive](/docs/free-srd/core-rules/vehicle-rules/#vehicle-actions) and does it at a *minor disadvantage*; a dedicated gunner is not driving and does not. And because a ship is more than two size categories above anything on foot, a hit against a person uses the [1d12 damage die](/docs/free-srd/core-rules/size/) and counts as [extreme](/docs/free-srd/core-rules/combat/#damage-sources) damage of its type — an automatic 1 VIT loss per die, whether or not DEF reached zero. Shooting people with a ship works. It is not subtle and it is not survivable.

> [!WARNING]
> **Ship-to-ship combat is not covered by these rules yet.** Everything above resolves a ship acting on people, docks and terrain, which is where a party actually meets one. A sustained exchange between two vessels needs a subsystem that does not exist on this site, and the honest answer for now is that it is the GM's scene to run.
